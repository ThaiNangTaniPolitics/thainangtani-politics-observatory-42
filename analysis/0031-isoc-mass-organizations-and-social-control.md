## 0031 – Mass Organizations: Who Is Organised, Who Is Paid, Who Reports
### *ISOC's Social Layer in the Documentary Record*

*Last updated: 12 October 2026, 12:49:33 (ICT)*

Thailand's internal security apparatus reaches into villages through state-sponsored volunteer organisations. Some of them are salaried, some are not; some carry a written mandate to gather information. This node documents which organisations exist, who pays their members and on what legal basis, and where surveillance appears as a stated task. It closes with a 2017 ISOC document that links a cabinet-approved payment to the question of using the recipients as sources of information.

*Correction (October 2026): The earlier version of this node carried no sources. Its section on the Village Scouts is removed. The "007 Intelligence Networks", the "reporting of unusual activities", and the statements that mass organisations operate "outside bureaucratic oversight" and "through loyalty rather than legality" are withdrawn for want of a source (Section 7).*

---

## 1. Scope and Method

Evidence levels:

- **Level 1** — official instruments: the Finance Ministry regulation on Volunteer Defence Corps remuneration (Royal Gazette, 2018), a cabinet resolution of 20 January 2015, a provincial administration letter of 2025, and official explanatory pages of the Department of Provincial Administration (DOPA) and of the Secretariat of the House of Representatives.
- **Level 2** — scholarship: Puangthong Pawakapan, *Infiltrating Society* (ISEAS–Yusof Ishak Institute, 2021), read in the original; the Pro-Government Militias Database (PGMD), Sabine Carey and Neil Mitchell, University of Mannheim, data to the end of 2014.
- **Level 3** — an official document known only from its reproduction in the press: an ISOC Region 2 memorandum of April 2017, printed as images by Kom Chad Luek.
- **Level 4** — press reporting.

## 2. The Organisations

| Organisation | Thai abbreviation | Origin | Responsible body | Size (as of, source) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volunteer Defence Corps (VDC) | Or Sor (อส.) | 1954, VDC Act | Ministry of Interior (DOPA) | 25,925 in the southern border provinces (2018; Puangthong, p. 95). No national figure found. |
| Village Security Teams | Chor Ror Bor (ชรบ.) | 1975 village self-defence programme under ISOC; statute 1979 | DOPA; ISOC selects the villages | 463,471 in 76 provinces, Bangkok excepted (28 Nov 2023; House Secretariat). Programme in 14,328 villages, armed teams enlarged from 10 to 30 per village (2017; Puangthong, p. 123). |
| Thai National Defence Volunteers (TNDV) | — | 1978, regulation of the Office of the Prime Minister | ISOC directly | 217,238 members (2011; Puangthong, p. 123). |
| Rangers | Thahan Phran (ทหารพราน) | 1978, Army | Army; Navy | 23,227 contracted (21,118 Army, 2,109 Navy) (Jan 2015; cabinet resolution). |
| Reservists for National Security (RNS) | — | 1981 (Army), under ISOC from 1986 | ISOC | 658,915 trained 1982–1992 (Puangthong, pp. 99–100). |

Puangthong: *"Members of the state-supported mass organizations can change their hats according to the state's demands"* (p. 104). The same people can appear under different organisational names.

## 3. Surveillance as a Stated Task

- **The rationale.** *"State surveillance over the remote areas would never be effective unless the government could recruit loyal villagers who had a comprehensive knowledge of the local area and could keep a constant watch over their neighbours in a way that outside authorities could not"* (Puangthong, p. 93).
- **TNDV.** The tasks listed in the founding regulation include *"suppressing riots and other disturbances, undertaking surveillance for intelligence gathering"* (Puangthong, pp. 98–99; the regulation itself not read).
- **Earlier units.** Of a programme set up in 1969: *"All of them simultaneously worked as spies for ISOC"* (Puangthong, p. 98).
- **Coding in the PGMD.** "Intelligence gathering" is coded as a purpose of the Village Security Teams (entry 249) and of the Rangers (entry 254). For the Village Security Teams the database cites a 2011 report of the Justice for Peace Foundation that lists intelligence gathering among their duties.

---

## 4. Who Is Paid

**Volunteer Defence Corps — salaried.** Finance Ministry Regulation No. 8 B.E. 2561 replaced the remuneration table with 33 steps, from **4,870 baht** (step 1) to **16,790 baht** (step 33), in force from 1 October 2017 (Royal Gazette Vol. 135, Special Part 162 Ng, 10 July 2018, pp. 1–3; Level 1). Any increase beyond the table requires Finance Ministry agreement (clause 10). The Ministry of Interior's spokesperson gave the monthly range including the cost-of-living supplement and support payment as 10,000 to 16,790 baht, plus a field allowance of 200 baht a day (Dailynews, 20 December 2024; Level 4). In 2025 one step was added for at most 15 % of members in service (Sakon Nakhon provincial letter of 21 March 2025; Level 1).

**Rangers — on contract.** Rangers serve on four-year contracts. The cabinet raised their field allowance from 120 to 200 baht per person per day with effect from 20 January 2015 (cabinet resolution; Level 1).

**Village Security Teams — unpaid by rule.** DOPA:

> ชรบ. จะไม่มีค่าตอบแทนหรือค่าเบี้ยเลี้ยง หรือผลประโยชน์อื่นใด เนื่องจาก เป็นประชาชนหรือราษฎรอาสาสมัครในพื้นที่

*"Chor Ror Bor receive no remuneration, no allowance and no other benefit, because they are local citizens and volunteer residents"* (DOPA Internal Security Affairs Bureau, FAQ, 22 April 2021; Level 1). The House Secretariat repeats that there is no salary, allowance or remuneration (2024; Level 1). Payment occurs by special programme: for 32,740 Chor Ror Bor along the Cambodian border the Interior Minister announced 120 baht per duty day of 6–12 hours and 240 baht above 12 hours (Bangkokbiznews, 9 August 2025; Level 4; the decision itself not found).

**Coding in the PGMD.** Members paid: "yes" for the Volunteer Defence Corps and the Rangers; "no information" for the Village Security Teams.

**Side income.** According to an interview Puangthong conducted with an Interior Ministry official (Chiang Mai, 29 November 2017), armed VDC and TNDV members earn extra money at sports events, political rallies and weddings (p. 133; Level 2, single interview).

*Puangthong's salary figures for the VDC (pp. 132–133) differ from the Royal Gazette and are not used here.*

## 5. Payment and Information: The ISOC Region 2 Memorandum (2017)

**The payment.** Under Prime Ministerial Order 66/2523 (1980), communist fighters who surrendered were designated "participants in developing the Thai nation" (ผู้ร่วมพัฒนาชาติไทย) and were to receive farmland. Because land could not be provided, the promise was converted into cash from 2005. Successive governments paid in rounds (Kom Chad Luek, 30 April 2017; Level 4):

| Government | Per person | Recipients |
|---|---|---|
| Surayud (2007) | 125,000 baht | 2,609 |
| Abhisit (paid before the 2011 election) | 225,000 baht | 9,181 |
| Prayut (2017) | 225,000 baht | 6,183 |

The cabinet of General Prayut Chan-o-cha's government approved **1,391,175,000 baht** from the central budget for 6,183 recipients. ISOC screened the claimants: in 2013 it reported some 130,000 claimants nationwide, of whom 4,430 were accepted as genuine (Kom Chad Luek; Level 4). The money came from the central budget, not ISOC's own line — the pattern described in 0034, Section 4.

**The memorandum.** A record of a meeting held on 31 March 2017, addressed to the Director of ISOC Region 2, printed by Kom Chad Luek as three page images (Level 3). The document number and day are blank; it is a draft or unsigned copy. Region 2 (the Northeast) accounted for 1,365 recipients and 307,125,000 baht. Under the chair's directives, item 1.4.3 reads:

> ๑.๔.๓ การจัดพิธีมอบเงินช่วยเหลือ ควรจัดพิธีภายในค่ายทหารและเรียนเชิญ ผวจ. ที่เกี่ยวข้องมาร่วมพิธี […] และควรอำนวยความสะดวกและดูแลผรท. ให้ดีเฉกเช่นนักรบผู้กลับใจมาพัฒนาประเทศ ให้ได้ใจ และ ได้มวลชน จากนั้นทุก กอ.รมน.ภาคควรพิจารณาว่าจะใช้มวลชนผรท. มาเป็นแหล่งข่าวหรือใช้เป็นมวลชนในการสนับสนุนกิจกรรมของทางรัฐบาลได้อย่างไรในอนาคต

*"1.4.3 The ceremony for handing over the assistance should be held inside an army camp, with the provincial governors concerned invited to attend […] and the participants should be accommodated and well looked after, like fighters who have turned back to develop the country, so as to win their hearts and win the mass. Thereafter every ISOC regional office should consider how the mass of participants could in future be used as sources of information, or as a mass in support of government activities."*

The same item cautions against favouring only this group, because Rangers would then also claim state care.

**Limits.** The wording is a recommendation ("should consider"), not an order. The document is known only from its press reproduction. Whether any regional office acted on it is not documented. Puangthong cites the same article (p. 136).

---

## 6. What the Record Does Not Establish

- It does **not** show that all mass-organisation members are paid. The largest group, the Village Security Teams, is unpaid by rule.
- It does **not** show that members generally act as informants. Surveillance is a stated task for the TNDV and a coded purpose for two groups in the PGMD; the 2017 memorandum concerns one group and recommends that the question be considered.
- It does **not** support any claim about intent beyond the documents' own words.

---

## 7. Findings and Limits

**Documented:**
- VDC members are paid on a 33-step table of 4,870 to 16,790 baht set by Finance Ministry regulation (Level 1).
- Rangers serve on four-year contracts with a field allowance of 200 baht a day (Level 1).
- Village Security Teams, 463,471 strong, receive no standing remuneration (Level 1).
- Surveillance for intelligence gathering is a task in the TNDV's founding regulation (Level 2) and a coded purpose of the Village Security Teams and the Rangers (Level 2).
- In 2017 an ISOC Region 2 document recommended that every regional office consider using recipients of a 1.39-billion-baht cabinet payment as sources of information (Level 3).

**Withdrawn for want of a source:**
- The "007 Intelligence Networks".
- "Reporting of unusual activities" as a TNDV function (replaced by the regulation's wording, Section 3).
- That mass organisations operate "outside bureaucratic oversight" and "through loyalty rather than legality".

**Open:**
- The text of the 1978 TNDV regulation and of the 2008 Interior Ministry regulation on Village Security Teams.
- The current VDC table after 2018 and the rule that lifts the minimum to 10,000 baht.
- A national membership figure for the VDC.
- Whether the 2017 recommendation was implemented.
