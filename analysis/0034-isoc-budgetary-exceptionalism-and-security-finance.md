## 0034 – ISOC's Budget: Status, Size, Spending Through Other Agencies
### *What ISOC's Budget Is, Where It Sits, and What the Published Record Leaves Open*

*Last updated: 9 October 2026, 07:47:57 (ICT)*

ISOC has its own line in the national budget, roughly ten billion baht a year by the late 2010s. Its activities are also financed from the budgets of the agencies it directs. Since 2017, the Budget Bureau has been bound to allocate funds in line with ISOC's approved plans. This node documents the budget status, the size of the line, the spending that runs through other agencies, and the scale of state spending in the southern border provinces. It separates what the published record shows from what it leaves open.

*Correction (October 2026): Sections 1–6 of an earlier version of this node carried no sources. They asserted "classified intelligence budgets", "off-book operational spending", the bypassing of procurement rules, budgets "conditioned on ideological alignment", and a weakening of budget oversight by the Internal Security Act. These statements are withdrawn (Section 7). The figures on the southern border provinces, added in August 2026, are retained as Section 6.*

---

## 1. Scope and Method

Evidence levels:

- **Level 1** — statutory text: the Internal Security Act B.E. 2551 (2008), Royal Gazette Vol. 125, Part 39 Kor; English wording from the unofficial translation of the International Commission of Jurists.
- **Level 2** — Puangthong Pawakapan, *Infiltrating Society: The Thai Military's Internal Security Affairs* (ISEAS–Yusof Ishak Institute, 2021), read in the original, printed pages. Her budget table is drawn from the Budget Bureau's expenditure budgets.
- **Level 4** — press and civil-society reporting: The Standard, iLaw, and The Nation (Section 6).

---

## 2. Budget Status

- **A budget agency by statute.** *"ISOC shall be a government agency according to the law on budget procedures and the law on government finance"* (§ 6). Its budget is part of the annual budget act that Parliament passes.
- **Before 2008.** While ISOC rested on administrative orders, *"the Bureau of the Budget did not allocate an annual budget directly to the agency"*. Under Prime Ministerial Order No. 158/2545 it had to submit projects through the agencies it planned to work with (Puangthong, p. 56).
- **Internal rules.** The Internal Security Operations Board, chaired by the Prime Minister, issues *"regulations concerning budget, financing, properties, and the management of the assets of ISOC"* (§ 10(3)).
- **Special remuneration.** Officials serving in an area designated under section 15 *"อาจได้รับค่าตอบแทนพิเศษตามที่คณะรัฐมนตรีกำหนด"* — *"may receive special remuneration as determined by the Cabinet"* (§ 22, Royal Gazette, p. 42).

## 3. Size of the ISOC Budget Line, 2009–2018

| Fiscal year | Baht |

| 2009 | 8,222,878,800 |
| 2010 | 8,240,272,500 |
| 2011 | 8,306,068,400 |
| 2012 | 6,915,923,000 |
| 2013 | 7,980,125,500 |
| 2014 | 8,201,570,700 |
| 2015 | 8,906,478,600 |
| 2016 | 10,200,971,600 |
| 2017 | 6,357,934,200 |
| 2018 | 10,049,512,900 |

Source: Puangthong, Table 2.3, p. 56, from the Budget Bureau's expenditure budgets for fiscal years 2009–2018 (Level 2).

For comparison, Puangthong gives the 2018 budgets of Thailand's two core national security organisations: 304.9 million baht for the National Security Council and 760.1 million baht for the National Intelligence Agency (p. 56, citing the Budget Bureau). ISOC's line was about nine times the two combined (10,049.5 against 1,065.0 million baht).

The fall in fiscal year 2017 coincides with the consolidation of southern-border spending into a single integrated plan in the same year (Section 6.2). Whether the two are connected is not documented.

---

## 4. Spending Outside ISOC's Own Line

- **Costs borne by other agencies.** Puangthong: *"The true budget for ISOC's activities was much higher than the above figure. When ISOC sends orders to other agencies, those agencies have to use their own budget, even when carrying out ISOC-supervised activities"* (p. 56). Her example is an ISOC educational programme for students: the Ministry of Education paid for it, and ISOC provided the speakers and trainers.
- **Budget alignment since 2017.** According to iLaw, order of the head of the NCPO No. 51/2560 amended section 7(2) so that the Budget Bureau allocates funds in line with ISOC's cabinet-approved plan, and the agencies concerned draw up their programmes and projects accordingly (Level 4; the order itself not read). The mechanism is set out in 0033, Section 2.

ISOC's own line therefore understates what is spent under its direction. The total is not published in one place.

---

## 5. Parliamentary Scrutiny: What Is Documented

ISOC's budget does come before Parliament. In February 2020 opposition MPs cited, in a no-confidence debate, ISOC documents submitted to the House committee on the fiscal year 2020 budget bill. ISOC's spokesman, Maj. Gen. Thanathip Sawangsaeng, confirmed on 27 February 2020 that these were official documents. He said the House budget subcommittee had asked ISOC to prepare a report on its operations for fiscal years 2017–2019 as input for the 2020 budget (The Standard, 27 February 2020; Level 4). The dispute in Parliament concerned what the money had been used for, not whether Parliament could see the budget. Details in 0035, Section 7.

## 6. The Southern Border Provinces: The Numbers (added 23 August 2026)

The following figures concern state spending on the southern border provinces across agencies. ISOC is the lead agency there; its share is not published.

### *6.1 The headline sum*

Over **22 fiscal years (2004–2025)** the Thai state spent **510.365 billion baht** on the southern border provinces. The highest single-year allocation was **30.5128 billion baht in 2016**, under the National Council for Peace and Order (NCPO).

**A limitation that must travel with this figure:** it is *southern-unrest spending across agencies*, not ISOC's own budget. No public breakdown by agency was found. The figure therefore documents the scale of the envelope in which ISOC operates — not ISOC's share of it. Any use that presents 510.365 billion as ISOC's budget is wrong.

### *6.2 Consolidation that did not consolidate*

For fiscal year 2017 the NCPO gathered the spending into a single category, the **Integrated Southern Border Provinces Conflict Resolution Plan**. Investigations subsequently found that conflict-related funds were **also embedded in other budget plans**.

This is the one documented instance of conflict spending dispersed across budget plans. It is a specific, dated, sourced case — and it means **510.365 billion is a floor, not a total.**

### *6.3 What is itemised, and what is not*

| | baht | share |
|---|---|---|
| Total, 22 fiscal years | 510.365 bn | 100 % |
| Compensation to victims (as of 3 Jan 2025) | 4.4688 bn | **0.876 %** |
| — of which for death and bodily harm | 3.4119 bn | |
| — of which for property damage | 1.0568 bn | |
| **Not itemised in the reporting** | **505.896 bn** | **99.1 %** |

Stated precisely: the published reporting itemises one line. The remaining 505.896 billion appears as a lump sum. This is a statement about the published record, not a claim that no breakdown exists anywhere.

### *6.4 The decoupling*

| | incidents | deaths |
|---|---|---|
| 2007 — peak violence | 1,407 | 892 |
| 2020 — lowest year | 57 | 36 |
| 2025 | 150 | 48 |
| Total 2004–2025 | 10,116 | 5,999 |

**Violence peaked in 2007. The budget peaked in 2016 — nine years later**, by which time incidents had already fallen steeply. Since 2020 incidents have risen again by a factor of **2.63** (57 → 150).

### *6.5 The concession that has to be made, and why it costs nothing*

Between 2007 and 2020 incidents fell **95.9 %** and deaths **96.0 %**. Any argument that the spending "did not work" collides with those figures immediately and loses. **Concede them.**

The defensible position is narrower and stronger: the peak of spending arrived nine years after the peak of violence and coincided with the lowest year of the conflict, and the 505.896 billion that would settle the question of attribution has never been itemised. **The success claim and the failure claim are equally unprovable, and by the same missing document.** That condition — not a demonstrated failure — is what 22 years and half a trillion baht have produced.

### *6.6 Who dies*

Civilians are the majority of the dead in both published series: **3,644 of 5,936 (61.4 %)** as of February 2025, **3,661 of 5,999 (61.0 %)** as of January 2026. Of the injured, 6,442 of 13,129 (49.1 %) were civilians.

### *6.7 Four death tolls for one conflict*

| source | figure | as of |
|---|---|---|
| Nation Thailand, on government data | 5,936 | Feb 2025 |
| SBPAC, via Nation Thailand | 5,999 | Jan 2026 |
| AFP | "more than 7,000" | Aug 2026 |
| Reuters | "more than 7,800" | Aug 2026 |

The state's own centre reports a figure roughly 1,800 lower than the wire services, for the same conflict and the same start date. No published account reconciles them.

### *6.8 What these figures do not establish*

- They do **not** show that ISOC's funding depends on the incident count. ISOC produces the incident tallies and is the lead agency for southern security spending; **no source examined links the two.** The closed-loop reading is an inference, not a finding, and must not be presented as one.
- They do **not** establish classified or discretionary budget categories; the earlier version of this node asserted such categories without a source. Only the FY2017 embedding (§6.2) is documented.
- They do **not** support any claim about intent.

## 7. Findings and Limits

**Documented:**
- ISOC is a budget agency under the law on budget procedures (§ 6).
- Its own line ranged from 6.4 to 10.2 billion baht a year in 2009–2018 (Budget Bureau, via Puangthong).
- Agencies carrying out ISOC-supervised activities pay from their own budgets.
- Since 2017 the Budget Bureau is bound to allocate in line with ISOC's plan (per iLaw).
- Officials in section-15 areas may receive special remuneration (§ 22).
- Southern-border spending across agencies reached 510.365 billion baht over 22 years, 99.1 % of it not itemised in the published reporting (Section 6).

**Withdrawn for want of a source:**
- "Multiple research studies".
- "Classified intelligence budgets" and "off-book operational spending".
- The bypassing of procurement rules.
- Development funds tied to "security compliance" or "ideological alignment".
- A weakening of budget oversight by the Internal Security Act. The Act contains no budget secrecy or audit exemption; its jurisdiction rule (§ 23) concerns courts, not budgets.

**Interpretation:** that ISOC's financing sustains its role as the back-end of a dual governance system (0037).

**Open:**
- ISOC's budget lines after 2018, from the Budget Bureau's expenditure budgets.
- The breakdown of ISOC's draft budget for 2027, including the share booked as "other expenditure", which was disputed in the Thai press in July 2026.
- The text of order 51/2560.
