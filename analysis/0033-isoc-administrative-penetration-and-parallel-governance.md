## 0033 – Administrative Penetration and Parallel Governance
### *How the Internal Security Act Places Civilian Administration Under ISOC*

*Last updated: 9 October 2026, 05:06:35 (ICT)*

ISOC does not need to infiltrate civilian administration covertly. The Internal Security Act B.E. 2551 (2008) and its amendment by order of the head of the National Council for Peace and Order in 2017 set out, section by section, how ministries, provincial governors and civil servants are drawn into ISOC's planning and command structure. This node documents that structure from the statute and from sources that describe it. Its interpretation as the back-end of a dual governance system is set out in 0037.

*Correction (October 2026): an earlier version of this node carried no sources. It stated that the Internal Security Act allows ISOC to bypass parliamentary budget scrutiny, the administrative courts and local council oversight. The Act does not support these statements as made (Section 5). The node has been rebuilt.*

-----

## 1. Scope and Method

Evidence levels:

- **Level 1** — statutory text. The Act is cited from the Royal Gazette (Vol. 125, Part 39 Kor, 27 February 2008) and from the unofficial English translation of the International Commission of Jurists. Quoted English wording is from that translation.
- **Level 2** — Puangthong Pawakapan, *Infiltrating Society: The Thai Military's Internal Security Affairs* (ISEAS–Yusof Ishak Institute, 2021), read in the original, printed pages.
- **Level 4** — iLaw, *"ผลงาน 'มาตรา 44' ขยายงาน 'กอ.รมน.' วางฐานอำนาจทหารหลังยุค คสช."* *("The work of 'Section 44' expands ISOC and lays the base of military power after the NCPO era")*, 4 July 2019. The 2017 order itself was not read.

## 2. The Legal Mechanism: Plans That Bind Ministries

Section 7 of the Act gives ISOC two linked powers:

- **(2)** *"to propose a plan and directions for operation and implementation for the Cabinet to consider and approve, and when the Cabinet has given approval, government agencies shall follow this plan and directions"*;
- **(3)** *"to direct, coordinate, and support the activity of government agencies in operations related to implementation under (2), pursuant to which the Cabinet may also assign ISOC the power to oversee implementation by government agencies as determined by Cabinet."*

Once the cabinet approves an ISOC plan, civilian agencies are bound by it, and ISOC can be given supervisory power over them. Puangthong notes that the Act removed earlier obligations to submit ISOC's plans and budget to the National Security Council: *"ISOC's policy and plans are approved by the cabinet, and other agencies must follow them"* (p. 58).

**Staff.** Under section 9, a government agency *"shall, at the request of the Director of ISOC, send state officials to serve at ISOC"*, and the agency receives replacement posts.

**The 2017 amendment** (order of the head of the NCPO No. 51/2560 under section 44 of the interim constitution, per iLaw; Level 4):

- It added disaster prevention and relief to the definition of internal security (§ 3).
- It required the Budget Bureau to allocate funds in line with ISOC's approved plan, and required the agencies concerned to draw up their own programmes and projects in line with it (§ 7(2)).
- It added the words "coordinate" and "both inside and outside the Kingdom" to ISOC's monitoring duty (§ 7(1)).
- It provided that officials seconded to ISOC keep their position allowances (§ 9).

The amendment was made by order of the junta leader, not by an act of parliament.

-----

## 3. The Territorial Structure: Army Commanders Above Governors

- **Regional ISOCs (§ 11).** Where internal security requires it, a Regional ISOC is established within an army region. *"The Commander of the Regional Army shall be the Regional Director of Internal Security."* The director may have civil servants of government agencies in the territory appointed to the Regional ISOC and *"shall have command over government servants, officers, and employees who have been ordered to work within the Regional ISOC."*
- **Provincial ISOCs (§ 13).** A Provincial ISOC is established as *"a unit reporting directly to the Regional ISOC"*; *"the Provincial Governor shall be the Provincial Director of Internal Security."* The governor, the head of the provincial civil administration, thus reports in internal security matters to the regional army commander.
- **Historical continuity.** The arrangement dates from the revised Anti-Communist Activities Act of 1969, which made governors provincial ISOC directors *"under the supervision of regional directors, posts held ex-officio by the commanders of the army regions"*. Puangthong concludes: *"This means civilian agencies are under the power of the military in all matters concerning internal security"* (p. 33). The Thaksin administration placed the provincial directors under the prime minister. The 2008 Act *"restored the previous ISOC structure, which placed the provincial directors under the commanders of the army regions"* (p. 58).
- **Provincial committees since 2017.** According to iLaw, order 51/2560 created an internal security committee for each Provincial ISOC with 38 members. It includes the provincial prosecutor, the deputy military director of the Provincial ISOC and representatives of most ministries present in the province (interior, social development, agriculture, natural resources, energy, commerce, labour, education, public health), as well as the army area responsible for the province (Level 4).
- **Mass organisations.** As ex-officio provincial ISOC directors, *"all governors must report TNDV activities to the regional directors or army Region Commanders"* (Puangthong, p. 98).

## 4. Assessment by a Thai Security Scholar

iLaw reports that Surachart Bamrungsuk, writing in Matichon, described ISOC after the 2017 amendment as a *"ซุปเปอร์กระทรวง"* (*"super ministry"*) in Thailand's state administration, with a mission covering every field regardless of politics (Level 4; the Matichon article itself not read). This is his assessment, attributed as such.

## 5. Oversight: What the Act Provides and What It Does Not

The earlier version of this node stated that the Act lets ISOC bypass parliamentary budget scrutiny, the administrative courts and local council oversight.

- **Budget.** ISOC is *"a government agency according to the law on budget procedures"* (§ 6). Its budget passes through the annual budget act. What the 2017 amendment changed is the opposite direction: the Budget Bureau is bound to allocate in line with ISOC's plan (Section 2). The size and composition of ISOC's budget are treated in 0034.
- **Courts.** Section 23 assigns cases arising from Chapter 2 measures to the Courts of Justice instead of the administrative courts. Chapter 2 measures are the special powers that require a cabinet resolution under section 15. The rule does not cover ISOC's standing duties under section 7. Puangthong explains why the redirection matters, citing the International Commission of Jurists: *"The Administrative Courts can review an order or terminate official actions at any time, even before their full impact is felt. But the Courts of Justice have jurisdiction only over a single case after a person has suffered some form of harm"* (p. 58). Wording of section 23: 0035, Section 5.
- **Local councils.** No provision of the Act concerns elected local councils. The statement that ISOC bypasses them is withdrawn for want of a source.

The documented point is narrower than the earlier claim, and more precise. The Act does not exempt ISOC from oversight in general. Instead, it binds civilian agencies to ISOC's plans, places governors under army commanders in internal security matters, and moves review of special-power measures to courts that act only after harm has occurred.

-----

## 6. Findings and Limits

**Documented:**
- Cabinet-approved ISOC plans bind government agencies (§ 7(2)).
- ISOC can be given supervisory power over their implementation (§ 7(3)).
- Agencies must second staff on request (§ 9).
- Regional army commanders head Regional ISOCs and command the civil servants assigned to them (§ 11).
- Governors head Provincial ISOCs that report to the Regional ISOC (§ 13).
- Since 2017, the Budget Bureau and the agencies concerned must align their allocations and projects with ISOC's plan (order 51/2560, per iLaw).

**Withdrawn:**
- "Override local administrative decisions".
- "Influence brokers" and "gatekeepers for resource distribution".
- "Local budgets tied to loyalty-based criteria".
- "Classified spending lines".
- The bypassing of parliamentary budget scrutiny and of local councils.

None of these had a source.

**Interpretation:** that this structure forms the back-end of a dual governance system that "runs through" the civilian front-end (0037).

**Open:** the text of order 51/2560 in the Royal Gazette; the current number and composition of Provincial ISOCs; the Matichon article by Surachart Bamrungsuk.
